A stand-in voice assistant talks to Veil's MCP server (POST /mcp, protocol 2025-11-25). It sees only what the room hears. The right-hand panels are operator instrumentation on a separate token-gated endpoint. Synthetic calendars; no Amazon service or device is involved.

  1. Assumes the default scenario (protected window = early prep); press new simulation first if you changed the selects. Ask is alice free for dinner, then is alice free for early prep. The second is refused: the transcript so far would narrow the protected window. Watch the protected-window bits stay at 0 on the right while the calendar-identity bits move - the room learned something, just not the protected fact.
  2. Compare the two grids now: the same conversation with Veil still leaves the protected window undetermined; without Veil it is already fully worked out.
  3. Click ask Alice to approve this (simulated) after the refusal, approve it on the right, and watch the protected-window bits move only now, by exactly the disclosed amount - the window is determined from here because Alice chose to disclose it.
  4. Rerun with a different calendar, protected window or declared prior from the scenario selects (and restart the script from step 1) - the guarded answers preserve whichever prior you declare.
  5. Joint protection: pick a second protected window (try late prep), restart, and ask can alice cover exactly one of the two prep windows. It is refused - the answer would expose the JOINT pattern even though each window's own margin would stay 50%. A per-window guard cannot see that; the pattern table on the right shows all four combinations still at their declared prior.
